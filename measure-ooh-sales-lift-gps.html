How to measure OOH sales lift with GPS data.

A GPS-tracked route tells you exactly where and when your ad was on the street. Lined up against your own sales, it turns a mobile billboard campaign into a test you can read. Here's the method, step by step.

By AOTC, LLC planning teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20264 min read

The short answer

To measure OOH sales lift with GPS route data: choose a sales metric and a baseline period, pick a test area and a similar control area, run the route only in the test area, then line up your sales by day, hour and location with the GPS-tracked route. Lift = (test after ÷ test before) ÷ (control after ÷ control before) − 1. Add a response code so you also have an exact floor.

Key takeaways

  • Decide the metric and the baseline before the campaign starts, not after.
  • Use a control area the route never enters, as similar to the test area as you can find.
  • The GPS-tracked route with times is what lets you match sales to the hours and streets the ad actually ran.
  • Calculate lift against both areas' own baselines so seasonality cancels out.
  • Treat response codes as the floor and the matched-area lift as the estimate.

1. Pick one sales metric and a baseline

Choose the number the campaign should move, such as transactions, revenue, new customers, calls or bookings, and the period you'll compare it with, usually the same weeks before the campaign and, if you have it, the same weeks last year.

Write both down before the truck rolls. Choosing the metric after you've seen the results is how campaigns end up "working" on paper.

2. Choose a test area and a control area

Pick two areas that behave alike: similar neighborhoods, similar store sales, similar seasonality. The campaign runs in the test area only. The control area gets nothing new, so it shows you what would have happened anyway.

If you have several locations, pair them up: each store in the test group gets a similar partner in the control group.

3. Plan the route inside the test area

Route the truck by daypart through the streets that feed the test locations, and keep it out of the control area entirely, including the drive to and from the route. A control area the truck crossed on the way to lunch is no longer a control.

4. Collect the GPS-tracked route with times

The route record shows where the vehicle was at every point of the run. It answers the questions a sales analysis needs: which blocks, which hours, how long beside each location.

AOTC sends the GPS-tracked route and photos of the ad on the street after every run. During the run, the Golden Ticket route room shows the truck live.

5. Line up your sales with the route

Put sales by day and hour next to the route's times for each location in both groups. A simple sheet works: one row per location per day, with the hours the truck was nearby, sales and calls.

ColumnWhere it comes from
Location and groupYour list: test or control
Date and hours exposedThe GPS-tracked route
Sales, transactions, callsYour point of sale and phone system
Code usesYour QR, promo code or call-tracking reports
NotesWeather, local events, promotions

6. Add a response code for an exact floor

Put one QR code, promo code or call-tracking number on the screens and nowhere else. Its count is exact but incomplete, because many people act without using it. It gives you a minimum to set beside the lift estimate.

7. Calculate the lift

Compare each group with its own baseline, then compare the groups: lift = (test after ÷ test before) ÷ (control after ÷ control before) − 1.

Example, for illustration only: if test-area sales rise from 1,000 to 1,150 transactions (up 15%) while the control rises from 1,000 to 1,030 (up 3%), lift is 1.15 ÷ 1.03 − 1, or about 11.7%. That 11.7% is the estimated extra business the campaign drove in the test area.

8. Repeat, then change one thing

One test is a reading, two are a pattern. Run the next flight with one change, such as a different daypart, message or neighborhood, and compare again. Over a few flights you learn which hours and streets pay back.

Questions

Quick answers.

01What does GPS route data show?

Where the vehicle was and when: the streets driven, with times, for the whole run. AOTC sends the GPS-tracked route and photos after every run, and clients can watch the truck live in the Golden Ticket route room.

02How long should a sales-lift test run?

Long enough for customers to act and for a normal week's ups and downs to even out. For a local business that usually means at least one full week of the campaign and a matching baseline period before it, plus a few days after.

03Is sales lift the same as ROI?

No. Lift estimates the extra sales the campaign drove. To get return on investment, multiply the extra sales by your margin and compare that with what the campaign cost.
