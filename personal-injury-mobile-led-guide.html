What personal injury firms should know about mobile LED.

Personal injury firms already know outdoor advertising. Mobile LED screens work differently from a highway board: they move, they change, and they put the firm at street level in specific communities. Seven things to know before booking one.

By AOTC, LLC planning teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20264 min read

The short answer

Before a personal injury firm uses mobile LED billboards, it should know that the state's lawyer-advertising rules apply (no misleading claims, and the ad must identify a responsible lawyer or firm), that placement must stay tasteful (never near accident scenes, hospitals or funerals), that number and name recall is the creative's main job, that language matters in the communities served, and that call tracking makes the results readable. Cost is quoted by route and hours.

Key takeaways

  • Lawyer-advertising rules apply to every screen: truthful, not misleading, with a responsible lawyer or firm identified.
  • Never route or park near accident scenes, emergency rooms or funerals; it's distasteful and can raise solicitation concerns.
  • The creative's job is recall: the firm name, the practice area and a number or short web address.
  • Run in the languages of the communities you serve.
  • A dedicated call-tracking number is the simplest way to see what the screens produce.

1. The advertising rules apply on every screen

Lawyer advertising is regulated by each state's rules of professional conduct, most of them modeled on the ABA Model Rules. In general:

  • No false or misleading communications (Model Rule 7.1), including implied guarantees of results.
  • Identify who's responsible: the name and contact information of at least one lawyer or firm (Model Rule 7.2).
  • Limits on solicitation of specific people (Model Rule 7.3).
  • State extras: some states require labels, disclaimers on past results or restrict testimonials and dramatizations.

Have the firm's ethics counsel or your state bar's advertising guidance check the creative. This guide isn't legal advice.

2. Placement must be tasteful

A truck can go almost anywhere, which is exactly why firms need a rule: never near accident scenes, emergency rooms, hospitals or funerals. Beyond being distasteful, appearing where people are in crisis can look like targeting specific victims. Route on commuter corridors, arterials and community streets instead.

3. Recall is the creative's job

People rarely need a lawyer the moment they see the ad. They need to remember the firm when they do. That means:

  • The firm name, large
  • The practice area in a few words ("Injured at work?")
  • An easy number or short web address, held on screen long enough to remember
  • Consistency with the firm's TV, radio and highway boards, so every exposure builds the same memory

4. Language and community

Serve communities in the languages they speak. A digital screen can alternate English and Spanish (or Polish, or another language) in the same loop. Routes through the neighborhoods the firm serves, at the hours people are out, beat a generic downtown loop.

5. How it differs from a highway billboard

 Highway billboardMobile LED
WhereOne spot on one roadChosen streets and neighborhoods, by hour
CommitmentUsually four weeks or moreHours or days
MessageOne, until reprinted (static)Can change by daypart or language
Share of screenYours (static) or rotating (digital)Rotates with no competing firm; or yours alone on an exclusive booking

6. Cost

Published industry rate guides put an LED billboard truck at roughly $1,200 to $3,600 per day, usually with a four-hour minimum. AOTC quotes the exact route and hours, usually the same day.

7. Track the calls

Use a call-tracking number only the screens show, ask every caller where they heard of the firm, and compare call volume from the areas the truck drove with the weeks before. AOTC's GPS-tracked route shows exactly where and when the ad ran.

Questions

Quick answers.

01Are mobile billboards allowed for law firms?

Yes, lawyer advertising is allowed in every state, subject to that state's rules of professional conduct. The same rules that govern a firm's TV spots and highway billboards apply to a mobile LED screen.

02What must a law firm ad include?

Rules vary by state, but most follow the ABA Model Rules: no false or misleading statements, and the ad must include the name and contact information of at least one lawyer or firm responsible for it. Some states add labels or limits on testimonials and past results. Check your state bar's guidance.

03How do personal injury firms measure mobile billboard ads?

With a phone number or short URL used only on the screens, intake questions about where callers saw the firm, and a comparison of call volume from the areas the truck drove against the weeks before.
