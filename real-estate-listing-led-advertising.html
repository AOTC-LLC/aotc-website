Mobile LED advertising for real estate listings.

A listing gets a sign in the yard and a page online. A few deserve more: the luxury home, the open house that needs a crowd, the agent building a name in a neighborhood. How agents and brokerages use mobile LED screens, and the rules that come with it.

By AOTC, LLC planning teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20264 min read

The short answer

Real estate agents and brokerages use mobile LED screens to drive open-house traffic, give luxury and new listings a launch moment, farm a neighborhood with repeated presence, win listing appointments by showing sellers a bigger marketing plan, and recruit agents. Ads must include the information state license law requires (usually the brokerage name) and follow fair-housing advertising rules. Put the address, the open-house time and a QR code to the listing on screen.

Key takeaways

  • Open houses are the strongest use: the truck routes traffic to the door in the hours before and during.
  • A full-motion screen shows a luxury listing's photos and video at a size that stops people.
  • Repeated presence in one neighborhood builds an agent's name for future listings.
  • Showing sellers a street-level launch plan can help win the listing appointment.
  • Include the brokerage name and follow fair-housing rules on every screen.

Open houses: the strongest use

Most open-house visitors come from the neighborhood and the main roads into it. Route the truck along those roads in the two hours before the open house and the first hour of it, with the address, the time and an arrow-simple direction. Park or circle nearby during the event where it's allowed. Count sign-ins against the brokerage's usual open-house traffic.

Luxury and standout listings

A full-motion LED screen can show a listing's best photos and video at a size no yard sign can. For a luxury home or a unique property, a launch weekend on the routes of likely buyers, and through the neighborhood itself, makes the listing an event, and the photos of it make good marketing for the agent too.

Farming a neighborhood

Agents build listings by being the familiar name in a neighborhood. A few days a month on the same streets, with the agent's face, the neighborhood name and recent results, keeps the name in front of future sellers between mailers and door knocks.

The rules on every screen

  • Brokerage name. State license laws, Illinois's included, generally require licensees' ads to include the sponsoring brokerage's name.
  • Fair housing. No wording or imagery that states or implies a preference or limitation based on protected characteristics.
  • Accuracy. Prices, features and "sold" claims must be current and true.
  • Seller consent. Get the seller's approval before featuring their home and address.

Your managing broker has the final word on compliance.

Creative that works

  • One great photo or a short video loop
  • "Open today 1–4" or "Just listed," and the street
  • A QR code to the listing page, large enough to scan
  • The agent and brokerage name

Questions

Quick answers.

01Do mobile billboards help sell homes?

They help the marketing around a sale: they bring traffic to open houses, give a listing a visible launch and build an agent's name in a neighborhood. Like all listing marketing, results show in showings, open-house sign-ins and inquiries.

02What must a real estate ad include?

Rules vary by state, but license laws, Illinois's included, generally require the sponsoring brokerage's name on licensees' ads, and the federal Fair Housing Act bars ads that state or imply a preference based on protected characteristics. Check with your managing broker.

03How long should an open-house run be?

Usually the two hours before the open house and the first hour of it, on the main roads into the neighborhood and the busiest nearby weekend corridors.
