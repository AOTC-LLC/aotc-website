Belmont Cragin Vintage Fest × AOTC

Vintage Fest didn't use AOTC as a moving poster. It used the truck as the event's advance signal — first sweeping the surrounding streets to build awareness, then locking into the entrance to turn arrival into a spectacle. At 100% share of voice, the festival owned every screen, every pass, and every moment the truck was in public view.

5020 W Fullerton Ave, ChicagoJune 6, 2026Community Event

The Story

The festival refused to wait for people to find it

The Risk

The biggest threat wasn't a weak event — it was quiet streets. A festival tucked into one address gets missed by people only a few blocks away.

The Move

AOTC took the message into motion, repeatedly putting the name, date, time, and location in front of the neighborhood instead of hoping a social post would do the work.

The Payoff

Once awareness was built, the truck stopped being an ad and became part of the event — adding color, scale, and a visual pulse that made the fest feel active before guests stepped inside.

Activation Flow

From curiosity to arrival

01

Interrupt the Neighborhood

The mobile route targeted busy streets around Belmont Cragin, where passing traffic couldn't scroll past or mute the message. Every pass created another chance to notice the fest and decide the turn was worth it.

02

Make the Entrance the Payoff

At the venue, AOTC shifted from awareness engine to atmosphere — a bright arrival marker confirming that something worth seeing was happening, reducing uncertainty and making the event feel active from the sidewalk.

The Creative System

The artwork didn't whisper

Two creative jobs. The short-form banner was built to punch through traffic and plant the name fast — a bright visual stamp recognizable from a moving car. The full flyer closed the gap: Saturday, June 6, noon to 6 PM, 5020 W Fullerton. It sold the experience — curated vintage, local vendors, free entry, summer vibes — not just the address.
