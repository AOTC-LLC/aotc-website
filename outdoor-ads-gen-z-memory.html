What makes outdoor ads stick with Gen Z.

Gen Z grew up skipping ads, so the ones they remember earn it. The creative traits that make an outdoor ad stick with people born after the mid-1990s, and the mistakes that make them look away.

By AOTC, LLC planning teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated November 4, 20264 min read

The short answer

Outdoor ads stick with Gen Z when they feel like a moment rather than a message: a self-aware or funny line, a reference to the exact place or event, a visual worth filming, and a brand that doesn't try too hard. Motion helps; so does a reason to share it. In a 2021 OAAA and Harris Poll survey, two-thirds of Gen Z and millennial respondents recalled seeing outdoor ads on social media, which is where a memorable street ad keeps working.

Key takeaways

  • Gen Z remembers outdoor ads that feel like a moment, not a sales pitch.
  • Self-aware humor and plain honesty beat borrowed slang.
  • Naming the place, the event or the neighborhood makes the ad feel like it's for them.
  • Make it worth filming: motion, a reveal or a joke that lands in a phone frame.
  • Two-thirds of Gen Z and millennial respondents in a 2021 OAAA and Harris Poll survey recalled seeing OOH ads on social media.

Why Gen Z is a hard audience, and a good one

Gen Z has skippable ads, ad blockers and endless feeds. They've learned to tune advertising out. That's exactly why outdoor matters: it's one of the few places an ad can't be skipped. But not being skippable only gets a glance. Getting remembered takes creative that earns the second look.

Six traits of outdoor ads that stick

1. It feels like a moment

The ad that shows up outside the concert as the crowd lets out, or names the game in progress, feels like part of the night instead of an interruption. Timing and place do half the creative work.

2. It's honest, and a little self-aware

A brand that admits it's an ad, or jokes about itself, reads as confident. Borrowed slang and fake authenticity read as trying too hard.

3. It's specific

Naming the neighborhood, the campus, the train line or the event tells people the ad was made for them. Generic national lines are easy to ignore.

4. It moves

A reveal, a countdown or a loop that rewards watching to the end holds attention on a full-motion LED screen in a way a static board can't.

5. It's worth filming

If someone would pull out a phone to show a friend, the ad keeps working long after the truck drives on. Design so the joke and the brand both fit in a phone frame.

6. It's easy to act on

A QR code big enough to scan, a handle or a short URL. No paragraph of instructions.

What makes Gen Z look away

  • Slang that's already dated by the time the ad runs
  • Feature lists and fine print
  • Brand logos with no idea attached
  • Claims that sound too good to be true
  • The same ad, unchanged, in every city and every hour

The social afterlife

Outdoor and social aren't separate for Gen Z. In a 2021 survey by the OAAA and The Harris Poll, two-thirds of Gen Z and millennial respondents said they recalled seeing outdoor ads on social media. A street ad that's filmed and posted reaches far more people than the ones who were standing there. Plan for it: make the ad worth posting, and repost the best clips yourself.

Questions

Quick answers.

01Does Gen Z pay attention to billboards?

They pay attention to outdoor ads that are worth it. Because outdoor can't be skipped or blocked, a clever or relevant street ad gets a real look, and the best ones end up filmed and shared on social.

02What tone works for Gen Z outdoor ads?

Honest, a little self-aware and specific. Gen Z audiences tend to spot ads that borrow their slang or fake authenticity. Say the true thing about the product in a way that's fun to repeat.

03Are mobile billboards good for reaching Gen Z?

They can be, because a mobile LED screen can be at the campus, the concert or the nightlife strip at the right hour, with full motion that's worth filming.
