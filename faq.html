The basics

01What is a mobile billboard truck?

A mobile billboard truck, also called an LED billboard truck, digital billboard truck or advertising truck, is a truck with built-in digital screens that drives your ad along a route you choose. The screens on both sides and the back carry the full ad (AOTC's own truck also has a front screen); it plays still images or full-motion video, and runs on GPS-tracked routes.

02How does LED billboard truck advertising work?

You tell AOTC your goal, audience, city and dates. AOTC plans a route and schedule around where that audience will be, such as the business district at rush hour, the ballpark on game day or a festival entrance, designs or fits your creative for each screen, and runs it in a rotation with no competing brand, or alone on the truck if you book it exclusively. Every route is GPS-tracked, and you get photos of your ad on the street after each run. You can preview your ad on the truck first in the free Display Studio.

03Who offers mobile digital billboards nationwide?

AOTC, LLC (Ads of the Century) plans and runs mobile digital billboard truck campaigns across the U.S. from its home base in Chicago, with market pages for 49 metros and campaigns across the lower 48. Campaigns are rotational, with no competing brand in your rotation, every route is GPS-tracked, and you get photos after each run. See every market.

04How is mobile billboard advertising different from a fixed billboard?

A fixed billboard waits in one spot and is usually sold by the month. A mobile billboard goes where your audience is, can change location and message by time of day, can run for a single day, and with AOTC is never shared with another advertiser on a standard booking. Compared with social video, it can't be skipped, blocked or scrolled past. More in mobile billboard vs. billboard.

05What kinds of businesses and organizations use mobile billboards?

Almost every kind: national restaurant brands, retailers, radio stations, real estate agents, industrial B2B companies, auto shops, nonprofits, labor unions, campaigns, entertainers and festivals. What they have in common is a moment they needed people to notice. See where the team has run campaigns.

Cost & booking

01How much does a mobile billboard cost?

It depends on how many hours and days you run, how much of the city the route covers, the market and the creative you need. Published industry rate guides put LED billboard trucks at roughly $1,200 to $3,600 per truck per day, usually with a minimum of about four hours. AOTC quotes include route planning and creative, and AOTC campaigns benchmark at about a $4–7 CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions), compared with $10–30 for social video. Text (773) 708-1400 for your dates.

02Do mobile billboards charge by the hour?

Many do, usually with a minimum of about four hours. Ask whether driving to the start of the route counts as billable time, and whether route planning and creative are included.

03What moves the price of a mobile billboard?

Hours and days are the biggest lever: a focused four-hour window around a moment can do more than a full day spread thin. The market, the daypart and season (rush hour, game days, festival weekends, holiday shopping), how complex the route is, the creative, share of voice and extras such as travel to another city, on-site time or more trucks all move it too. A rotation shared with other brands costs less than an exclusive booking, and AOTC never puts a competitor in your rotation.

04How do I compare two mobile billboard quotes?

Ask both the same questions. Is the screen mine alone, or shared in a rotation? Is the route planned for me by daypart, or is it a standard loop? Is creative included, and can the message change during the run? What counts as billable time, and what is the minimum? What record do I get? A GPS-tracked route and photos of your ad on the street should be standard. And how do rescheduling and cancellation work in bad weather?

05How do I work out a mobile billboard's CPM?

CPM is the cost per thousand impressions: the price divided by the impressions, times 1,000. If a day costs $2,000 and the route delivers an estimated 400,000 impression opportunities, the CPM is $5. Street impressions are estimates, so ask how any number was worked out and treat it as a planning figure, not a guarantee.

06Is a mobile billboard cheaper than a regular billboard?

For a short, date-driven campaign, usually yes in total spend, because you can buy one day or one event instead of a four-week period. Over a full year on one road, a fixed board can cost less per month. Compare by cost per thousand impressions and by what each one can actually do for your goal.

07What's included in an AOTC quote?

The route plan, the hours, still-image creative designed in-house, a rotation with no competing brand, a GPS-tracked route and photos of your ad on the street after every run. Motion design and extras such as other cities or on-site time are quoted with the plan. Send the city, the dates, the hours, who you want to reach and whether you have creative, and AOTC comes back with a route plan and a quote, usually the same day.

08How long does an LED truck campaign run?

As long as the goal needs. A single-day activation can own a grand opening, a festival or a game day. A multi-day flight builds the repetition that turns recognition into recall, and longer runs keep a brand present in a neighborhood. AOTC schedules the hours and days your audience is actually on the street.

09How far in advance should I book an advertising truck?

As early as you can, especially for holidays, festivals, trade shows and summer weekends, when dates fill quickly, and earlier still for campaigns outside Chicago or across several cities. AOTC has also turned campaigns around fast for real deadlines, including an event booked the day before, so text (773) 708-1400 with your date to check availability.

10Is the truck available for private events?

Yes. The four LED screens also work as an event screen for brand activations, festivals, corporate events, watch parties, movie nights, birthdays and memorials. At an Elevated Exotics event in New Buffalo, the screens ran movies and video games live. See LED screen truck rental.

Routes & targeting

01Can I choose where a mobile billboard drives?

Yes. Every AOTC route is planned for one client. Name the streets, neighborhoods, venues or events, and AOTC builds a loop and schedule around when your audience is there.

02Can a mobile billboard target a specific neighborhood or event?

Yes. A route can be as tight as a few blocks around a new location or a festival entrance, or as wide as several neighborhoods in a day. See event and festival advertising and downtown routes.

03Can a mobile billboard run near a stadium on game day?

Yes. AOTC has run Cubs Opening Day routes through Wrigleyville for American Precision Services, timed to pre-game arrivals and post-game crowds, and plans the same timing around stadiums in any city. See game-day advertising.

04How do you plan routes around my target audience?

AOTC starts with who you need to reach and where their day happens: the corridors they commute, the blocks they walk, and the venues and events they go to. From there it builds a loop and schedule that keeps the truck in front of that audience at the hours they're there: wide coverage for a launch, tighter repeated loops when a date matters. More in how to plan a campaign.

05Do you run at night?

Yes, and night is often when the screens do their best work: full-motion LED stays bright after dark, when fixed boards fade. Evening routes suit nightlife, entertainment, events and any brand whose customers are out after work.

05

Screens & creative

01What artwork or video files does an LED billboard truck need?

A JPG, PNG or MP4 for each screen: 1920 × 1029 px for the two side screens, 1080 × 1014 for the back and 1280 × 641 for the front. No artwork? AOTC designs it in-house, or preview your own design on the truck for free.

02How big are the screens?

Each side screen is 11.76 × 6.30 ft and the back screen is 6.71 × 6.30 ft, so the full ad reads from both sidewalks and the traffic behind. AOTC's own truck adds a front screen above the cab, 6.71 × 3.36 ft.

03What if I don't have creative ready?

Then we make it. AOTC designs creative in-house, and much of what runs on our screens was designed by us for the client. Bring an idea, a logo or nothing at all, or mock one up yourself in the Display Studio.

04Can I change my message during the campaign?

Yes. The screens are digital, so creative can change between dayparts, between cities on a tour, add a limited-time offer mid-run, or switch from "Opening Saturday" to "Now open" without a reprint.

05Do I share the screen with other advertisers?

Yes, on a standard booking, and that's what AOTC specializes in. Up to six brands take 10-second turns on the screens, never two from the same category, which keeps the price down; your share of voice runs from about 15% to 100%. If you want the truck to yourself, an exclusive booking puts only your brand on every screen. Your quote always says which one you're getting.

Results

01How do you measure a mobile billboard campaign?

In two parts: delivery and response. Delivery: every route is GPS-tracked, and after each run you get photos of your ad on the street. Response is measured the way you'd measure any channel: put a QR code, promo code, vanity URL or tracked phone number in the creative, then compare traffic, calls, sales or branded search in the route areas against the weeks before.

02How do I know the truck actually ran?

Every AOTC route is GPS-tracked, and after each run you get photos of your ad on the street, ready to share with your team or your client. Clients can also follow their truck live in the Golden Ticket route room.

03What kind of results can I expect?

That depends on your offer, your market and the moment you choose, which is why every AOTC plan starts with the outcome you want. Clients have used routes to fill grand openings, keep a cause in public view, put a radio station in front of a festival crowd and win a holiday weekend at a highway exit. Put a code or link on the screen and you can count the response yourself.
